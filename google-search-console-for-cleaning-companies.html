Google Search Console for Cleaning Companies: Five Reports Worth Checking

By Nakul Chandra, Founder and Lead Strategist · November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

Google Search Console is a free tool that shows how your website appears in Google search. It is one of the most useful tools a cleaning company can use, and most owners have never opened it. You do not need to learn everything. Five reports cover most of what matters, and our local SEO work starts here.

Performance: what people search to find you

This report shows the searches that led to your site, how many times you appeared and how often people clicked. Look for searches with many impressions and few clicks. Those are pages that rank but need a better title or description.

Pages: which pages Google has indexed

The Pages report shows which URLs are in Google and which are not, with reasons. New sites often have pages waiting to be crawled. If an important page is excluded, find out why before assuming anything is wrong.

Sitemaps: tell Google what exists

Submit your sitemap once so Google knows about every page. It does not guarantee ranking, but it helps new pages get discovered faster. Your sitemap usually lives at the root of your site, such as /sitemap.xml.

URL Inspection: check a single page

Paste any URL to see whether Google has it, when it last crawled it and whether it can be indexed. For new or updated pages you can request indexing, though there is a daily limit, so save it for important pages.

Core Web Vitals and mobile usability

These reports flag slow or awkward pages on real phones. Most cleaning customers search from a phone, so a slow site costs leads. Fix the worst pages first, usually heavy images and unused scripts.

What Search Console is and is not

Search Console is a free tool from Google that shows how your site appears in Google Search. It does not show visitors on your site, which is Analytics' job. It shows impressions, clicks, average position, indexing problems and what Google could and could not read. For a local cleaning company, it is the most direct window into what Google thinks of your pages.

Set it up correctly

Verify the right property, ideally a URL-prefix or domain property covering your whole site, and submit your sitemap. Make sure both the www and non-www versions redirect to one version, so data is not split. Once connected, give it a few days to start collecting data.

Reading the Performance report

Look at queries that bring impressions but few clicks. These often point to a page title or description that does not match what searchers want. Look at pages with many impressions and a low position, because a small improvement in content or links might move them onto the first page. Filter by country and by device to understand where your visitors are.

Fixing indexing issues

The Pages report lists URLs Google found but did not index. Common reasons are duplicates, redirects, noindex tags or thin content. Not every page needs to be indexed, but important service and city pages should be. When a key page is missing, inspect it, fix the cause and request indexing.

Using it alongside Analytics and your profile

Search Console tells you how people reached you, Analytics tells you what they did and your Google Business Profile tells you how they found your listing. Use all three together, as explained in our GA4 guide and our local SEO page.

What to do with the data each month

Check queries with high impressions and low clicks, pages that lost impressions, and any new indexing errors. Update titles and descriptions for pages with weak click rates. Add useful content to pages that are close to the first page. Record changes with dates so you can see what worked.

Search Console for new pages and blog posts

After publishing a new service page, city page or article, inspect the URL and request indexing, then add internal links from related pages. Check back in a week or two. For a new site, make sure the sitemap is submitted and the robots file does not block important pages.

Common problems and what they usually mean

Crawled but not indexed often means a page is thin or duplicate. Discovered but not indexed can mean the site is new or low priority. A drop in impressions may come from seasonality, a competitor or a technical change. Investigate before changing things. See our local SEO service if you want help interpreting the reports.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Google Search Console free?

Yes. It is free for any website owner who can verify ownership.

How do I verify my site?

You can verify with a file upload, a meta tag, your DNS provider or an existing Google Analytics tag, depending on how your site is hosted.

Why is my page not indexed yet?

New pages can take days or weeks. Check the Pages report for a reason, make sure the page is linked from other pages and is in your sitemap.

Can Search Console fix my rankings?

No. It shows what is happening so you can act. The improvements still come from better content, links, reviews and technical fixes.

How long until Search Console shows data?

Often a few days after verification, and fuller data builds over weeks.

Should I request indexing for every page?

No. Submit your sitemap and use URL Inspection for important new or changed pages. Over-requesting does not speed things up.

What is a good average position?

It depends on the query. Positions vary by searcher and location, so use the figure to compare over time, not as a target.

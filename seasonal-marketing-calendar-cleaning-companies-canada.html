A Seasonal Marketing Calendar for Canadian Cleaning Companies

By Nakul Chandra, Founder and Lead Strategist · November 11, 2026 · 6 min read

Cleaning demand is not flat. Spring brings deep cleans, summer brings moves, and winter brings salt, slush and holiday guests. A simple seasonal plan lets you prepare pages, offers and ads before demand arrives instead of scrambling once it does. The ideas below are a starting point, so adjust them with your own booking data.

Late winter to spring: deep cleaning and spring refresh

Many households start thinking about a deep clean as the snow melts. Prepare a deep cleaning page, update your Google Business Profile photos and consider a seasonal offer. If you offer carpet or window cleaning, this is a natural time to promote it.

Summer: moves and move-out cleaning

Moving peaks in summer in much of the country. In Quebec, July 1 is the traditional moving day, which makes late June a busy time for move-out cleaning in that market. Make sure your move-in and move-out pages are strong before the rush.

Fall: back to routine and commercial contracts

As people settle back into routines, recurring home cleaning and commercial contracts come up for review. This is a good time to push office and janitorial pages and to follow up on quotes you sent earlier.

Winter: salt, mud and holiday hosting

Winter wears on floors and carpets, and holiday hosting creates one-off cleaning demand. Promote carpet, floor and pre-holiday cleaning, and make sure your holiday hours are updated on your profile.

Build the page before the season

Search engines need time to find and trust a page. Publish seasonal pages and posts a month or two before you need them, then refresh them each year instead of starting again. Our services can handle this planning for you.

Why a calendar helps

Demand for cleaning in Canada changes with the weather, school year and moving dates. If you plan ahead, you can publish pages, adjust ads and prepare staff before the rush rather than during it. A simple calendar also keeps you from over-spending in quiet months.

Plan around local dates

Moving seasons vary by region. Quebec has a well known July 1 moving date, many university cities peak at the end of April and August, and tourism areas peak in summer. Use your own booking history to mark your busiest weeks and plan back from them. Our city guides, such as Montreal and Edmonton, note some local patterns.

What to publish and when

Publish or update the page for each seasonal service four to eight weeks before demand starts, so it has time to be found. Refresh photos, update your Google profile with a seasonal post and prepare your ad copy. For commercial work, remember that budgets and contracts follow business calendars, not weather.

Match your staffing to your marketing

Do not advertise services you cannot deliver. If spring deep cleaning fills your schedule, tighten ad budgets and raise the quote threshold rather than overpromise. A short wait list is better than missed appointments, which hurt reviews.

Measure and adjust each year

At the end of each season, note which pages, ads and offers produced jobs and which did not. Keep a one-page record, and update it every year. Over time, your calendar becomes specific to your business. See our guide to tracking calls and quote requests for how to measure it.

A sample calendar to adapt

January to February: plan, update photos, review last year. March to May: promote spring deep cleaning, windows and carpets. June to August: focus on move-out cleaning, vacation rentals and recurring plans. September to October: promote back-to-routine cleaning, commercial contracts and fall deep cleaning. November to December: holiday hosting, post-party cleaning and gift certificates. Adjust each block to your region and customers.

Regional differences across Canada

Prairie and northern cities face long winters, with salt and dry air, while coastal cities deal with damp weather and tourism seasons. Quebec has its July 1 moving tradition, and university cities have predictable end-of-term turnovers. Read our city pages, such as Winnipeg and Vancouver, for examples of how markets differ.

Budget planning

Shift advertising spend toward the weeks before your busiest times, and keep a small baseline throughout the year. Hold back a reserve for quick changes, such as a cancelled event or a sudden cold snap. Review spend and results quarterly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a different website for each season?

No. Add or refresh pages for the services that matter in each season and keep the rest of the site stable.

When should I start Google Ads for a seasonal push?

Start a few weeks before demand rises so campaigns have time to learn. See our Google Ads service for how we plan this.

Does the calendar differ across Canada?

Yes. Weather, moving habits and holidays vary by province and city, so adjust dates for your market and check your own booking history.

Should I discount in the off-season?

It can help fill the schedule, but protect your margins. Many companies offer bundles or recurring plans instead of simply lowering price.

When should a cleaning company start spring marketing?

Often in late winter, a month or two before demand rises. Check your own history to confirm.

Is winter a slow season for cleaning?

It varies. Some residential demand slows, but holiday and post-event cleaning and commercial contracts often stay steady.

Should I discount in slow months?

Sometimes, but consider adding value or targeting recurring customers rather than cutting prices across the board.

Choosing an agency

Seven Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Marketing Agency for Your Cleaning Company

By Nakul Chandra, Founder and Lead Strategist · November 11, 2026 · 6 min read

Choosing a marketing agency is a big decision for a small cleaning company. Many agencies sell the same package to every industry, and cleaning is not like most industries. These questions help you find out whether an agency understands how cleaning companies win work. We are happy to answer every one of them. See our about page and pricing for a start.

Who will actually do the work, and who will I talk to?

Ask whether you speak to the person doing the work or to a salesperson. Small businesses do better with a clear point of contact who understands the service.

Have you worked with cleaning companies before?

Ask for live examples, not mockups. Look at the sites, then search for the company on Google. A real example shows what the agency can do. You can see ours on our work page.

Can you explain what you will do in plain language?

A good agency can describe the plan without jargon. If the answer is a pile of acronyms, you may not get what you think you are paying for.

What will you report, and how often?

You should see traffic, calls, form submissions and Google Business Profile activity, not just rankings. Reports should connect to leads, because leads are the point.

Do you guarantee rankings?

No honest agency can guarantee a position on Google, because Google makes the final call. Be careful with anyone who does. Ask instead what they will do and how they will measure progress.

Who owns the website, ad accounts and listings?

You should own your domain, website, Google Business Profile and ad accounts. If the agency owns them, leaving later can be painful. Get this in writing.

What does the contract look like?

Ask about the term, how to cancel and what happens to your assets if you leave. Month-to-month with clear deliverables is easier to trust than a long lock-in with vague promises.

Why these questions matter

Marketing for a cleaning company is a service you cannot see easily. Promises are cheap, and some agencies sell the same package to every industry. The questions in this guide help you test whether an agency understands your business and will treat you fairly. Ask them in writing if you can, so the answers are on record.

Ask to see work for similar businesses

Ask for examples of websites, profiles or campaigns for cleaning or home service companies, and for what the client was trying to achieve. Be cautious with screenshots of rankings and results that cannot be verified. A good agency explains the plan and the reasoning, not just the outcome.

Understand costs and what is included

Ask for a clear list of what the monthly fee covers and what costs extra, such as ad spend, content, tools and revisions. Ask whether there is a setup fee and what happens if you stop. Ad budgets should be separate from the management fee and paid straight to Google or Meta.

Red flags to watch for

Be wary of guaranteed rankings, secret methods, pressure to sign quickly, refusal to share access, packages that are identical for every client and agencies that will not explain their reporting. Also be careful if the agency owns your website, domain or advertising accounts.

How to compare two or three agencies

Write the same questions for each agency, ask for a short proposal and compare how clearly each explains the first ninety days. Choose the one that explains things plainly and fits your budget, not the one with the biggest promise. Our own approach is on our pricing page, and you can book a call to ask these same questions of us.

What a good first ninety days looks like

A good agency starts with an audit, agrees priorities with you, sets up tracking, fixes foundations such as your Google profile and key pages and then begins ongoing work. You should hear what they plan to do each month, see access to every account and receive a first report. Be wary of an agency that starts spending your ad budget before tracking exists.

Questions about reporting and communication

Ask who your contact is, how quickly they reply, how often you will meet and what the report will show. Good reports connect work to enquiries and explain what changes next. Reports that only list rankings or impressions say little about the health of the business.

Fit matters as much as skill

An agency may be skilled but wrong for you if it works only with large budgets, does not understand residential or commercial cleaning or communicates in jargon. Choose people you can work with for a year. Ask about their experience with Google Business Profile work and website design if those are your priorities.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should a cleaning company hire a niche agency or a general one?

A niche agency understands your customers, seasons and competitors, so it usually needs less explaining. A general agency can work too if they show relevant results.

How much should a small cleaning company spend on marketing?

It depends on your goals and market. Start with what you can sustain for several months, since SEO especially needs time. Our pricing page shows our packages in Canadian dollars.

How long before I see results?

Paid ads can produce leads quickly. SEO usually takes several months. Anyone promising instant first-page rankings is overpromising.

Can I switch agencies later?

Yes, if you own your assets. That is why ownership of the website, domain and accounts matters so much.

Should I hire a local agency or a national one?

Either can work. What matters is experience with businesses like yours, clear communication and transparent terms.

How long should I commit to an agency?

Be cautious about long contracts. Many services need months to show results, but you should still be able to leave with your accounts and assets.

Can I do some of this myself?

Yes. Many owners handle reviews and photos themselves while an agency handles technical and advertising work. Agree who does what.

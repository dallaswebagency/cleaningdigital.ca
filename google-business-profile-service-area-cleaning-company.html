How to Set Up Your Google Business Profile Service Area (Cleaning Companies)

By Nakul Chandra, Founder and Lead Strategist · November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

Most cleaning companies go to the customer, so there is no shop front for people to visit. Google treats that kind of business as a service-area business, and the way you set it up changes where you show up. A messy setup is one of the most common reasons a good cleaner stays invisible on the map. Our Google Business Profile service covers this setup end to end.

Hide your address if customers do not visit you

If you work from a home office or a shared unit, you do not need to show that address publicly. Google lets service-area businesses hide the address and show the areas they serve instead. Showing a home address you do not want the public to see is a privacy risk, and publishing an address where customers cannot actually meet you can cause suspensions.

List the areas you really serve

Add the cities and neighbourhoods where you take jobs. Google limits how many areas you can list, so choose the ones that make business sense, not every town within a two hour drive. If you only clean in Calgary and Airdrie, say that. A tight, honest list is easier for Google to trust.

Pick the right primary category

Your main category matters more than any other field. Choose the one that matches your biggest earner, such as house cleaning service or janitorial service, and add secondary categories for the other work you do. Do not add categories you do not offer.

Add services and write a clear description

List each service you offer, such as move-out cleaning, office cleaning or deep cleaning, with a short plain description. Use your real service names, not a long list of keywords. Then mention the same cities in your description so the profile and your website say the same thing.

Match your website to your profile

Your website should have a page for each core service and each main city, and your business name, phone number and service area should match your profile exactly. Link the profile to the best matching page, not always the home page. See how this fits into our local SEO service.

Why the setup matters more than most owners think

A service-area profile decides where you appear and how customers read you. If the address is wrong, the areas are vague or the category does not match your work, you can show up in the wrong searches or not at all. Google also checks profiles against its rules, and a profile that breaks them can be suspended, which takes the listing offline until it is fixed. A careful setup takes an hour or two and avoids weeks of trouble.

Common service-area mistakes

The most common mistakes are listing a long string of cities you do not actually serve, using a keyword-stuffed business name, showing a home address publicly when customers never visit, and leaving the phone number or website different from what is on your site. Each of these sends mixed signals. Keep the name exactly as it appears on your signage and invoices, and make every detail match your website.

Reviews, photos and posts after the setup

A profile is not finished once the fields are filled. Ask every happy customer for a review with a direct link, reply to every review politely, and add real photos of your team and finished work. Occasional posts about seasonal services show the business is active. Never buy reviews or offer rewards for them, because that breaks Google's rules. Our Google Business Profile service covers this work in detail.

Connect the profile to the right page

Link your profile to the page that best matches what the searcher wanted, which is often a service page rather than the home page. A visitor who clicks from the map should land on something that answers their question and offers a clear way to book. This is part of our local SEO work.

Check it every month

Open the profile each month, look at calls, website clicks and direction requests, and check that nobody has suggested an edit that changed your details. Spam edits and duplicate listings do happen. Keeping an eye on the profile catches problems early.

A short checklist before you save

Confirm the business name matches your signage, the phone number matches your website, the primary category fits your main service, the address is hidden if customers never visit, and every area listed is one you genuinely cover. Add at least a dozen real photos and a clear description of what you do. Then ask three recent customers for reviews. This checklist covers most of what separates a strong profile from a weak one.

Residential, commercial and specialty services

If you do both house cleaning and janitorial work, choose a primary category for the work that matters most and add secondary categories for the rest. List services separately with short descriptions, such as move-out cleaning, office cleaning or carpet cleaning. Customers searching for each will then find a matching entry. Keep descriptions plain and avoid listing every suburb inside them.

How profile and website support each other

A strong profile sends visitors to a website that must follow through. Make sure the page they land on has your phone number, a quote path, proof of insurance and a few reviews. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a physical address to have a Google Business Profile?

No. Service-area businesses can hide their address and list the areas they serve instead. You still need to be a real business that serves customers in person.

How many service areas can I add?

Google caps the number of areas you can list. Choose the cities where you actually take jobs rather than trying to cover everything nearby.

Will listing more cities make me rank in more places?

Not by itself. Proximity, relevance and reviews matter a lot. Listing places you do not serve can hurt trust, so keep it honest.

Should my profile link to the home page?

Often a more specific page works better, such as the service or city page that matches what people searched for. Test which page converts best.

How long does it take for service area changes to show up?

Changes can appear within hours or take a few days, and sometimes Google reviews them first. Avoid making many edits at once, because that can trigger a review.

Can I have more than one Google Business Profile?

Generally only if you have separate, staffed locations that customers can visit. A service-area business with one base usually has one profile.

What should I do if my profile is suspended?

Check the reason Google gave, correct the issue, such as a wrong address or a name that includes extra keywords, and submit a reinstatement request with supporting documents.

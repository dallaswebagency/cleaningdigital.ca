Google Ads for cleaning companies in Halifax

Paid search in Halifax is a numbers game played on a modest scale. Fewer advertisers compete than in the biggest cities, which can mean reasonable costs, but the pool of searches is also smaller, so a poorly aimed click costs you a larger slice of the budget. That is why we group campaigns by service and by area, not into one catch-all campaign. When you can see move-out cleaning separately from carpet cleaning, you can see which is earning enquiries and which is not.

The calendar shapes the account. End of term brings move-out searches from students and landlords, spring brings deep and window cleaning, and wet, salty months bring floor and entry mats cleaning. Newcomers settling into the region add searches of their own. We look at your own booking history first, then plan budgets to rise before those peaks and ease off in quiet weeks, so spend follows demand rather than a flat monthly guess. It is a simple idea, but many accounts still run at the same level all year without anyone asking whether that makes sense.

Location matters at the edges. Someone in Bedford and someone in Dartmouth are different searchers with different travel times for your crew. We set location targeting around where you genuinely work, exclude places you do not, and test communities as separate campaigns when the budget allows. Because a harbour and several highways separate parts of the municipality, an ad that promises a visit within the hour from the wrong side can cost you trust as well as money. We check that kind of detail with you before launch, because you know your travel times better than any map does.

Choosing the first campaigns for a modest budget

A smaller budget works best when it is concentrated. We usually pick one or two services with clear demand and good margin, such as move-out cleaning and recurring home cleaning, and build those properly before touching anything else. It is a patient way to work, but it protects you from paying for many small experiments at once on a budget that cannot support them.

Running six thin campaigns means none of them gather enough data to judge. Once the first campaigns show a clear pattern, we add another service, a second area or a commercial campaign, one step at a time, so you always know what each addition cost and produced. Each addition is tested against the earlier ones, so you can see whether the new campaign brought real enquiries or only moved them around.

Writing ads that match what people typed

Searchers can tell when an ad has nothing to do with their problem. A person searching for move-out cleaning wants to see move-out cleaning, a mention of the area and a clear way to get a quote. We write headlines and descriptions around the actual job, not generic claims. A short test of two or three ad versions shows which wording your Halifax customers respond to, and we keep the winners and retire the rest.

We also use ad extensions such as call buttons, location details and extra links to the pages that matter. Every claim must be something you can deliver, so we do not write about guarantees, discounts or response times you cannot honour. We also keep your ads consistent with your site, so a customer who clicks never feels they have arrived somewhere different from what was promised.

Landing pages that turn clicks into enquiries

An ad is only half the job. If the click lands on a crowded home page, the visitor has to hunt for the form. We send each campaign to a page built for that search, with the service, the area, a short explanation and a quote button in view. A quick look at speed, wording and the quote button usually reveals the fixes that matter most before any budget is spent.

This is where our website design service and ads meet. If your current pages are not ready for paid traffic, we recommend fixing them first, because sending money to a weak page only makes the problem more expensive. Even a modest improvement in how many visitors send a form can lower your cost per enquiry without touching the ad budget at all.

Negative keywords and weekly search term reviews

Some of the searches that trigger cleaning ads are not customers. People look for cleaning jobs, cleaning supplies, free tips or services you do not offer. Without care, a small budget leaks into these quickly. We keep an eye on seasonal terms as well, such as end of lease or spring clean, and add or remove them as the calendar changes.

We add negative keywords from day one and review the actual search terms regularly, removing wasteful ones and adding useful ones. This is routine work, but in a smaller market it is often the difference between an account that learns and one that quietly burns money. A short note in each monthly report lists what was removed and why, so the thinking behind the account is always visible to you.

Tracking calls and forms honestly

We set up conversion tracking for calls, form submissions and, where possible, booked jobs, so the account reports on enquiries rather than just clicks. If tracking is missing or broken, nothing else in the account can be judged fairly. Tracking also lets us tell a slow week from a broken form, which is a real risk when a phone number or booking tool is changed.

Our guide to tracking calls and quote requests explains the setup. We also ask you to tell us which enquiries became jobs, so we can steer toward quality, not just volume. Over a few months this feedback loop makes the account smarter, because we learn which kinds of enquiries become your best customers. Small, steady habits like this tend to matter more than one big push.

Working with SEO and your Halifax page

Ads and organic search help each other. Paid campaigns show quickly which phrases bring enquiries, and we use that to guide the pages we write for SEO. In the other direction, strong organic pages can lower how much you need to spend on the same searches. The two services share the same tracking, so you see one set of numbers rather than separate reports that never quite agree.

How quickly can ads start bringing enquiries?

Once the account, tracking and landing page are ready, ads can run within days, and early searches often arrive quickly. The first weeks are for learning, though, so expect adjustments to keywords, ads and targeting before the account settles into a pattern. We will tell you honestly if a budget looks too small to learn from.

Should I bid on my own company name?

Sometimes. A small brand campaign can stop competitors appearing above you when someone searches your name, and it usually costs little. We test it, check that it is not simply capturing customers who would have clicked your free listing anyway, and keep it only if it earns its place.

What if students call and never book?

Price shoppers and tyre kickers are normal in any market. We tighten ad wording so it sets expectations, add a short qualifying question to forms and track which calls become jobs. Over time we shift budget toward searches that bring serious enquiries. That filter keeps your crews on jobs worth the drive.

Do I need call tracking for Google Ads?

It is strongly recommended. Many cleaning customers phone rather than fill forms, and without tracking those calls look like nothing happened. Call tracking shows which ads and keywords produced real conversations, so decisions rest on facts instead of guesses. The extra step costs little and pays back quickly in clearer decisions.

Can I pause ads during a busy period?

Yes. If your crews are fully booked, we can reduce budgets or pause campaigns and bring them back when you have space. Paying for enquiries you cannot serve frustrates customers and wastes money, so we plan with you around your real capacity. Flexibility is one of the advantages of paid search over printed ads.

Can ads help me find commercial cleaning clients?

They can, with a separate campaign. Commercial buyers search differently and want scope, scheduling and insurance details, so we send them to a dedicated page with a site-visit quote form. These campaigns are usually small and targeted, and we measure them apart from home cleaning. It also keeps your commercial calendar separate from the busy residential seasons.

Who owns the Google Ads account?

You do. The account is in your name, ad spend goes directly to Google and you can see everything in it.

How much should a cleaning company spend on Google Ads?

It depends on your market, competition and goals. We agree a realistic starting budget on the discovery call and adjust from the results.

Problems we solve

Where residential cleaning companies lose enquiries.

Reviews sit unanswered and out of date

A profile with a few old reviews and no replies tells a nervous homeowner that nobody is paying attention. Silence after a complaint can look worse than the complaint itself. A steady trickle of recent, answered reviews does more for a maid service than almost any other single change you can make.

The site describes services but not the visit

Customers want to know what happens when your team arrives. Do you bring supplies, how long does a standard clean take, who will be in the home, what if something breaks. Pages that skip all of this leave people guessing, and guessing usually ends with a call to someone else.

Pricing is a complete mystery

Many owners refuse to show any pricing and then wonder why enquiries are such a poor fit. A short explanation of how you quote, covering bedrooms, bathrooms, pets, condition and extras, filters out the wrong jobs and makes the right ones more comfortable to send your way.

First-time customers rarely become regulars

Money goes into winning a first clean, then the second one is left to chance. Without a simple recurring offer, a thank-you message and a gentle reminder after a few weeks, you keep paying to replace customers you had already earned and quietly lost.

Everything depends on one booking method

If the only way to book is a phone call in office hours, you lose the evening browser. If the only way is a long form, you lose the impatient one. Offering several routes, call, text, short form and online booking, catches more of the people who are already on your site.

How residential cleaning companies win work online

Nobody wakes up wanting to research cleaning companies. A homeowner searches after a rough week, a new baby, a party on Saturday or a landlord inspection, usually on a phone and often at night. They type something plain like house cleaner near me or maid service, glance at the map and read a few reviews before they read a word you wrote. What they want to know is simple. Will this person be careful, kind and on time in my home? Your website and profile should answer that quickly, in ordinary language, without any jargon.

The first booking is rarely the real goal. A single clean pays for the time and the supplies, but the weekly or bi-weekly customer is what makes a maid service stable. So the website should explain how a first visit works, what a regular schedule looks like and how easy it is to move from one to the other. Customers who feel looked after in week one tend to stay for years, and they mention you to their neighbours without being asked.

Homeowners also search by situation, not only by service. A deep clean before guests arrive, help after surgery, a tidy-up before listing a house, regular upkeep for a busy family with two dogs. Each of those is a different worry and deserves its own page. A company that writes to those situations in plain words, with a few honest photos of real work, looks far more real than one that lists services as a row of icons. Every one of those situations is a chance to sound like someone who has done it before.

Finally, people compare. They open three or four companies, check the star rating, look for a phone number and notice who answers a message first. You do not need to be the cheapest, but you do need to be easy to reach and clear about what a visit includes. A quote form that takes a minute, a number that dials with one tap and a reply on the same day often settle the job before price is ever discussed. Speed and clarity are what people remember.

Opportunities

Where the growth is.

A profile that works like a storefront

Your Google profile is the first thing many homeowners see. Complete services, accurate service areas, real team and van photos and recent reviews turn it into a storefront that does a lot of the selling before anyone visits your site. See how we handle it in our Google Business Profile service.

Situation pages that match real searches

A page for pet households, one for post-renovation tidy-ups and one for pre-sale cleans each speaks to a person with a specific worry. They are easier to write well than generic service pages, and they give search engines something distinct to show for each search.

Recurring plans as the default

Present weekly, bi-weekly and monthly plans as the normal choice and one-time cleans as the exception. When the structure is clear and the switch is easy, repeat revenue stops being a matter of luck and becomes the plan you work to every month.

Referrals that are actually asked for

Most happy customers would recommend you if asked, and almost nobody does it unprompted. A short line on invoices, a follow-up text after the clean and a simple refer-a-neighbour note give that goodwill somewhere to go, at almost no cost to you.

Ads for the busy weeks

Spring, pre-holiday and school-start weeks bring a spike in searches. Targeted Google Ads aimed at the services and areas you want can capture that demand, as long as the landing page is ready to turn the click into a quote request.

Across Canada

Residential cleaning changes a lot between Canadian cities. In prairie cities, long winters bring salt and grit indoors, spring cleaning is a real season and many homes are detached with finished basements. On the coast, damp weather, older character homes and a large rental market shape the work. Ontario's big metros mix condo towers with older semis and new subdivisions, and the person booking might be a landlord, a tenant or a parent. Each of these deserves its own wording and photos, not one national message. A condo owner and a family in a detached home are not reading the same page.

Language and habits matter too. In Quebec and parts of the Atlantic provinces, a site that works properly in French, or in both languages, is a genuine advantage. Moving dates, school calendars and holiday traditions also change when demand peaks. A company in a university town sees a very different year from one in a commuter suburb, and the website should reflect the customers you actually serve rather than an average of everyone. Small differences like these, said plainly, make a page feel written for the person reading it.

Competition is the last piece. A big city has many maid services and a crowded map, so reviews, photos and a complete profile carry more weight. A smaller city may reward the company that simply looks organized and answers quickly. Our guide to move-in and move-out cleaning marketing covers the turnover buyer, who searches very differently from a regular household and needs a page of their own. That includes landlords and agents. Write that page for the landlord or tenant who needs it.

What should a maid service website include?

A clear list of what each type of clean includes, honest photos, recent reviews, the areas you serve, a simple explanation of how you quote and a phone number that dials with one tap. A short quote form and a page explaining the first visit usually do more than extra design flourishes.

How do I get more reviews without being pushy?

Ask right after a good clean, by text or email, with a direct link to your Google profile. Keep the message short and personal. Reply to every review you receive. Never offer rewards for reviews or write them yourself, as that breaks platform rules and customers can usually tell.

Should I show prices on my website?

That is your call. Many companies show a starting point or an explanation of what affects the quote, such as bedrooms, bathrooms, pets, condition and extras. It tends to attract better-fit enquiries and saves you time on calls that were never going to turn into bookings.

Is Google Ads worth it for a house cleaning business?

It can be, especially in busy seasons or when you are opening a new area. It works best once your site and profile are in decent shape, because paid clicks sent to a weak page waste money. We set the account up in your name so you always see the spend.

How do I turn one-time customers into regulars?

Make recurring plans the obvious choice on your site, then follow up after the first clean with a thank-you and an easy way to set a schedule. A reminder a few weeks later catches people who liked the result but forgot to rebook, and it costs almost nothing.

Do I need separate pages for each suburb?

Only where you genuinely serve and where there is something real to say. A page for each area with its own housing details and photos can help. Near-identical pages with only the place name swapped do not, and they can hurt your credibility with both customers and search engines.

Can you help with a bilingual website?

Yes. If your customers use French and English, we can build pages that work properly in both, with natural wording rather than a rough translation. Tell us which languages your customers use and we will plan the page structure, the forms and your Google profile around that.

How soon will I see more enquiries?

Profile and page fixes can change the quality of enquiries within weeks, while rankings usually take months to build. It depends on your city, your competition and your starting point. We will not promise a number, but we will show you the calls and forms each month so you can judge.

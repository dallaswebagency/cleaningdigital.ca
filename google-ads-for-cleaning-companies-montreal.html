Google Ads for cleaning companies in Montreal

Ads in Montreal live or die on targeting. Searches come from the island, from Laval, from the South Shore and from many places where your crew would never go. We draw the map around where you can show up on time, and we exclude the rest. Language targeting matters too: an English campaign should reach people who search in English, not everyone within a radius who happens to be nearby. Getting this right early saves more money than any later tweak to ads or bids, because wasted clicks add up quietly.

Demand moves with the lease calendar. Move-out and end of lease searches climb as June ends and fall away once July 1 has passed, so a campaign for that work needs a sharper budget for a few weeks and a plan for the quiet that follows. Regular home cleaning, deep cleaning and office work behave differently, so we give each its own campaign and its own landing page instead of mixing them. Planning that rhythm in advance means you spend when customers are searching and rest when they are not.

The English audience is the honest limit. Fewer English searches means that a small budget can run out of relevant clicks quickly, and that costs per click may not behave the way they do in an English-majority city. We do not promise a number of calls. We start with a modest test, read the search terms weekly and tell you plainly whether English ads are worth continuing or whether French ads are the better next step. Honest reporting on that question is part of the service, even when the answer is not the one you hoped for.

Setting up a campaign for one service at a time

We build campaigns around what you want to sell, not around everything you do. Move-out cleaning gets its own campaign, regular home cleaning another, and office cleaning a third if you want that work. Each has ads written for that customer and a landing page that matches the search. That structure also makes it easier to see which service deserves more budget as the season changes.

Starting narrow keeps the data readable. If move-out ads bring calls and office ads do not, you will see it in a month and can move budget accordingly. A single broad campaign hides that and tends to spend money on whatever Google finds easiest to show. You also see clearly which message and page combination earns the most calls over time.

Keywords and the searches you should block

Cleaning ads attract searches you do not want: people looking for cleaning jobs, do it yourself instructions, supply stores and free advice. We add negative keywords from day one and review the real search terms every week, adding more as odd ones appear. In a smaller market, a few wasted clicks matter more. This weekly habit is dull work, but it is where much of the saving in a paid campaign comes from.

We also watch for searches that look right but come from outside your area, or that clearly belong to a different service than the one you advertise. Cleaning terms in Montreal can overlap with commercial laundry, restoration and property services, so careful matching saves budget. A careful setup keeps your spend focused on people who might actually book you. We also keep the list of blocked words growing as new patterns appear.

Landing pages that match the click

Sending ad clicks to a homepage wastes them. A person who searched for end of lease cleaning should land on a page about exactly that, with a short form, a tap to call number and a clear list of what is included. We use pages from your site if they are ready, and build focused ones if not. This focus is also why we recommend a short form: customers on a phone rarely want to type much.

Our website design service can prepare those pages so they load quickly on a phone and show your phone number without scrolling. Before any budget goes in, we test the form and the call tracking, so every enquiry can be traced back to the ad that produced it. A fast, simple page often does more for results than a clever ad ever could.

Timing the move-out season without overspending

For July 1 work, we plan the campaign weeks in advance: ads written, landing page tested, budget schedule agreed. Searches begin before the final week, and many customers book early, so a start that comes too late means you miss the careful planners and meet only the panicked last minute crowd. We would rather spend a little on the right weeks than a lot on the wrong ones.

We set a daily cap and watch it closely. If the campaign is working, you can raise it with a clear idea of what you are buying. If it is not, you stop with limited loss. Once the rush has passed, we scale back and shift budget toward recurring cleaning. Careful pacing through the season is a big part of making move-out ads worthwhile.

Reading results in a smaller English market

We report calls, form submissions and the cost of each, not just clicks. In a smaller audience, a few calls can change the picture, so we look at whether they were good fits: the right area, the right service, a real customer. Quality matters more than raw numbers here. Numbers without context can mislead, so we always add a short note explaining what we think they mean.

We will not promise a number of leads or a cost per enquiry. If the data after a fair test period shows English ads are not paying their way, we say so. That is more useful to you than a report full of impressions that never turned into work. Honest numbers let you decide calmly whether to continue, adjust or stop.

How ads, SEO and your profile fit together

Ads bring enquiries quickly while organic search builds slowly, and the two share data. Search terms from your campaign show what customers type, which guides pages and profile services. Our local SEO service uses those findings so you do not pay for the same lesson twice. That cooperation between channels is where small budgets stretch furthest. Shared lessons mean you never pay twice to learn the same thing about your customers.

Your Google Business Profile also matters, because a clear listing with reviews improves how ads and map results look together. The ad account stays in your name, ad spend goes straight to Google, and we are added as managers, so you keep control throughout. Nothing is hidden, and you can look at the account whenever you wish. You can also ask us for a plain explanation of any line in the account at any time.

Should I target the whole island or only part of it?

Only the parts your crews can reach comfortably. Drive time matters more than the map outline. We set targeting by area, watch which places produce calls and tighten or widen it as the data comes in. Paying for clicks from places you will never serve is the first thing we remove.

How early should a move-out campaign start?

Several weeks before the late June rush, so the ads, page and tracking are tested before spend rises. Early planners book first, and starting during the rush means competing with everyone at once. We also plan how to scale spend back down afterwards. We then plan how to scale spending back down once the rush has passed.

Can I run ads for both Laval and the island?

Yes, but we keep them in separate location groups so you can see how each performs. Laval and the island behave differently in volume and in what customers ask for. Separate reporting also helps you decide whether travel to a suburb is paying off. Keep each location group's results separate so you can compare them fairly month to month.

What if my English budget gets used up quickly?

That is a sign the English audience is small, which is expected. We set a daily cap, tighten the keywords to the best searches and report honestly on what the spend produced. If the budget is simply too small for the audience, we say so and suggest alternatives.

Do call extensions work for cleaning in Montreal?

They can, especially on phones. A tap to call button under the ad lets a stressed tenant reach you instantly. We set call hours to match when someone really answers and track those calls separately from form requests, so you know which ones produce booked work.

Do you write the ads in French if I ask?

No. Our work is in English. If you want French campaigns, we can structure them and manage the account, but the ad text and landing page should be written by a fluent native speaker. That keeps your message natural and protects your reputation. We structure and manage the account either way, and we tell you what we cannot do.

Who owns the Google Ads account?

You do. The account is in your name, ad spend goes directly to Google and you can see everything in it.

How much should a cleaning company spend on Google Ads?

It depends on your market, competition and goals. We agree a realistic starting budget on the discovery call and adjust from the results.

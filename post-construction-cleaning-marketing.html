Problems we solve

Where post-construction cleaning companies lose enquiries.

Contractors cannot tell what you actually do

Many sites describe post-construction cleaning in a single line under a general cleaning list. A site manager needs to know whether you handle rough clean, final clean, window and frame cleaning, floor care and debris removal, and how those fit the building schedule. Without detail, they assume you are a house cleaner stretching into new territory.

Proof of safety and insurance is hard to find

Construction sites have rules. Buyers look for liability insurance, safety training and how your crew behaves on site. When this information is buried or missing, the buyer moves on to someone who states it plainly. Be careful to publish only what is true, current and documented.

Work depends on a handful of relationships

If three contractors send most of your jobs, losing one hurts. Marketing in this niche has to widen the pool, which means being found by contractors who are searching, and staying in front of the ones who already know you.

Quotes are slow and inconsistent

Every project is different, so quoting takes time. A vague contact form leads to endless questions, while a clear request form that asks for the right project details gets you to a proper proposal faster. Slow responses lose jobs in an industry that runs on tight timelines.

How post-construction cleaning companies win work online

Post-construction cleaning is not sold like house cleaning. The buyer is usually a builder, general contractor, renovator or developer with a deadline, a handover date and a client waiting to walk through. They are not browsing for a nice website. They want to know that you will show up, work safely around the site, finish on time and not leave a mess of their own to explain to the client.

That makes this a trust and relationship business. Most work comes from repeat contractors and referrals, so the website's job is to back up a recommendation, not to replace one. When a contractor's colleague says call these people, the next thing they do is check you online. If what they find looks thin, the referral loses power. We build the online side so it holds up when that moment comes, and we add search and outreach to reach contractors who do not know you yet.

How do post-construction cleaners get more clients?

Mostly through contractors, builders and renovators. A credible website, clear information about your stages and insurance, a complete Google profile and good references all help. Search and targeted ads can reach contractors who do not know you yet, while relationships keep repeat work coming.

What should a post-construction cleaning website include?

A clear description of the stages you handle, how scheduling works, what the crew does on site, your insurance and safety practices and a simple proposal request form. Real project photos, used with permission, add trust. Everything on the page should be accurate and current.

Is Google Ads worth it for post-construction cleaning?

It can be, because the buyers are often searching at the moment of need. Search volume is modest, so targeting and landing pages matter. We start with tracking and a focused campaign, review the results with you and adjust. We do not promise lead numbers.

Should I list my prices online?

Most companies do not publish fixed prices, because every project differs. It helps to explain what affects the quote, such as size, stage, finishes and access, and to make requesting a proposal quick. That sets expectations without locking you into numbers.

Do I need separate pages for each city?

Only for cities where you genuinely work and can say something useful. Copying one page with a new city name does not help customers or search engines. We help you decide which areas deserve a page, and list the rest as service areas.

Can you also market my regular commercial cleaning?

Yes. Many post-construction companies also offer commercial or janitorial services. We keep them on separate pages so each buyer sees what matters to them, and link the two where it makes sense. See our commercial and janitorial page.

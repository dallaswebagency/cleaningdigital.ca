Problems we solve

Where pressure washing companies lose enquiries.

Your best work is hidden

Pressure washing sells on the visual. Many sites bury results in a tiny gallery or use photos taken in poor light with no context. A visitor cannot tell whether that is concrete, brick or stained cedar, so they cannot picture the result on their own property.

One page tries to cover every surface

A single Services page that lists decks, driveways, siding, fences and roofs in one paragraph will not match what people search for. Each surface has its own concerns, from sealing to soft washing, and a thin page signals that you do not specialise in anything.

The spring rush swamps the schedule

When the weather turns, calls arrive in a wave and slow ones get ignored. Without an intake form that sorts jobs, a missed call quickly becomes a lost customer who simply tries the next number on the list.

Quotes take too long to produce

If every enquiry needs a site visit before a number can be given, many people will not wait. They message three companies and book whoever replies first with something concrete, even if that is only a range.

Commercial buyers cannot find proof you can handle scale

Property managers want evidence you have worked on larger surfaces and carry proper insurance. A residential-looking site with nothing about lots, buildings or schedules makes them assume you only do driveways. Even a short line about the biggest surfaces you have handled would close that gap quickly.

How we work

How we market pressure washing companies.

1. A gallery that sorts by surface

We build a project gallery filtered by driveway, deck, siding and so on, with matched before and after pairs, short captions and a quote button beside every set. Visitors can find work that looks like theirs in seconds. Each album stays short, so the page loads quickly on a phone and the best work stays at the top.

2. Pages for each surface and method

Concrete, wood, vinyl and stone need different care. We write a page for each, covering what you do, what to expect and when soft washing is the better choice, linked with your local SEO plan. Each page also gets its own title, headings and photos, so none of them reads like a copy of the last.

3. A photo-first quote form

A short form takes an address, the surface type and up to a few photos. You reply with a range or book a visit, and the booking and quote setup keeps those requests in one place instead of scattered texts.

4. A profile that shows real jobs

We set up your Google Business Profile with your service areas, add job photos regularly and ask each happy customer for a review while the surface is still wet and the result is fresh. That keeps your profile looking active, which is often what tips a nervous customer toward calling.

5. Seasonal ad bursts

Short Google Ads campaigns for the weeks that matter, pointed at the right surface page and switched off when the schedule is full. We track calls and forms so the spend follows booked jobs. Each campaign gets its own landing page, so the visitor sees exactly the surface they searched for.

How pressure washing companies win work online

Nobody wakes up wanting a pressure washing visit. They notice green creeping across the north side of the deck, a grey driveway sitting next to a neighbour's clean one, or a letter from the condo board about the parking lot. Then they search, usually on a weekend with a phone in one hand. They look at photos first and words second. If your first screen shows a real before and after that proves the point, you are already ahead of the company using a stock picture of a man holding a wand.

Most of these jobs are decided by proof and by timing. A homeowner wants to see a driveway like theirs, cleaned, and wants a rough idea of what the job involves before anyone shows up. Property managers think about it differently. They want a company that can handle a whole lot, a loading dock or a building face, send a proper quote and turn up when promised. Your marketing has to speak to both without sounding like two different businesses.

The work is also seasonal in a way that most cleaning niches are not. Enquiries bunch up when the snow goes and the ground thaws, and again before fall sales, weddings and listings. People search with the surface in mind, such as driveway, patio, fence, siding or roof, so pages that match those searches tend to pull their weight. A single page called Services rarely does. That means a page for decks and a separate page for driveways will usually beat one page that tries to explain both, and each can carry photos of the right surface.

Quoting is the sticking point. Customers rarely know square footage, and many will not call until they have a ballpark. A short form that accepts a few photos and an address, plus a clear explanation of how you price, removes the guesswork for them and saves you a site visit on jobs that were never going to fit. That one change often improves the quality of enquiries more than any ad ever will. Add a line on typical turnaround and you answer the second question people always ask before they hit send.

Opportunities

Where the growth is.

Surface and service pages

A dedicated page for driveways, decks, siding, fences, patios and soft washing lets you match the words customers actually search. Each can carry its own photos, process notes and quote button, which also gives Google more to understand.

Before and after as your best sales tool

Consistent photos from the same angle, labelled with surface type and result, turn a gallery into proof. They also work as profile posts, ad images and social content without any extra effort. Aim for a handful of strong pairs per surface rather than hundreds of mediocre ones.

Seasonal timing

Pages and ads can be ready before the thaw, then shifted toward fall cleanup and pre-sale prep. Being visible in the first warm weeks, when people begin planning, is worth more than shouting in the middle of the rush.

Property managers and repeat contracts

Condo boards, retail plazas and strata managers need regular cleaning of lots, entrances and walkways. A simple commercial page explaining how you quote, schedule and insure the work can turn single jobs into annual contracts.

Neighbourhood word of mouth

One cleaned driveway on a street tends to prompt questions from the next door. Door hangers, a yard sign with your site and a tagged job photo on your profile help that curiosity reach you.

Across Canada

Pressure washing looks very different depending on where you work. On the Prairies, dust and road grime coat everything and the season is short once the frost lifts. In coastal British Columbia and the Maritimes, moss, algae and damp siding keep the work going well into the wet months. Ontario and Quebec sit between the two, with freeze and thaw cycles that crack old concrete and make sealing a common conversation. Along the Great Lakes and in the Maritimes, salt air and winter road spray leave their own film on garage doors and entrance steps.

Building types shape the offer too. Cities with older brick homes need gentle methods and careful mortar checks, while newer suburbs are mostly vinyl siding, stamped concrete and composite decks. Condo towers and strata complexes add parking structures and common walkways, which is a different quote, schedule and insurance question than a single driveway. Your site should reflect the surfaces common in the areas you serve. Planning your pages around those surfaces, rather than a generic list, makes customers in each area feel you already know what their street looks like.

Water access and local rules also change how you present the work. Some municipalities limit outdoor water use in summer, and many ask contractors to manage runoff near storm drains. A short note on how you handle both builds trust and sets you apart from the person with a borrowed machine. If you serve several towns, a page per area helps, using our Canada guide as a starting point. That area approach also lets neighbouring towns each see a page that mentions them by name. It is a small effort with a lasting payoff.

Do I need a separate page for each surface I wash?

In most cases, yes. People search for the surface they are looking at, such as a driveway, deck or fence, and a page for each lets you answer that search directly. It also gives you room to explain methods, show matching photos and add a quote button. Start with the two or three that bring the most work and add the rest over time.

How should I take before and after photos?

Stand in the same spot, at the same height and in similar light, and take the after shot as soon as the surface is dry enough to show the true colour. Add a quick note on surface type and what you did. Consistent pairs look more credible than dramatic ones, and they are easy to reuse across your site, profile and ads.

Is Google Ads worth it for a seasonal service?

It can be, if you treat it as a short burst and not a year round expense. Run it when demand is climbing and your schedule has room, send clicks to the matching surface page and measure calls and forms. When the diary fills up, pause it. We keep the budget small at first and only grow what produces booked jobs.

How do I get quotes without driving to every property?

Ask for the address, surface type and a few photos on your quote form, then give a range based on what you see. Many jobs can be priced this way, and you only visit when something looks unusual. Explaining how you price on your site also filters out people who just want the cheapest number.

Can I win commercial contracts as well as home jobs?

Yes, but those buyers look for different signals. A commercial page that covers lots, entrances, loading areas and building faces, plus a note on insurance and scheduling, tells property managers you can handle their site. Keep it separate from your home pages so each audience sees language that fits them.

What should my Google Business Profile show?

Your real service area, the surfaces you clean, current photos of finished jobs and your hours. Add new photos often, since a profile full of recent work reads as active. Answer every review politely. Customers searching on a phone will often decide from the profile alone, before they ever open your website.

When should I start marketing before the season?

Earlier than feels natural. Pages and your profile should be tidy before the thaw, and ads can begin as people start thinking about outdoor spaces. The company that is already visible when homeowners first look around often gets the call. Our seasonal marketing calendar lays out a sensible rhythm.

Will you help if I also do other exterior work?

Yes. Many pressure washing companies also offer gutter cleaning, window washing or sealing, and each deserves its own page and photos. We build the site around what you actually sell and make sure the services support each other, so a driveway customer can easily see what else you can handle.

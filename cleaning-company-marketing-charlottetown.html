Local guide

Marketing a cleaning company in Charlottetown.

Planning around a summer-heavy year

Summer visitors fill inns, cottages and rentals, and every checkout creates a clean. The cleaners who do best are the ones visible before the rush, not scrambling to update a website in July. Hosts and inn owners tend to line up their cleaners early, so spring is when many of those decisions are made.

We plan your pages, profile and offers a season ahead. Our seasonal marketing calendar shows when spring, summer and holiday searches tend to start, and we adapt it to an island where the calendar leans toward tourism. That usually means getting the turnover page and the profile ready before the first warm weekends, and using the shoulder months to promote residential and commercial work.

Turnover cleaning for hosts and managers

A cottage owner who lives off the island, or a host with several listings, wants a cleaner who is reliable, quick to confirm and easy to reach. They are not browsing for a home cleaner. They want a dependable turnover partner. They also care about photos of the finished result, a clear answer on same-day availability and an easy way to send a list of dates.

A dedicated page covering your scope, linen handling if you offer it, restocking, timing and how you handle access gives those customers confidence. See our move-in and move-out cleaning marketing guide for how that kind of page is built. We keep the wording plain and the quote form short, so a host can send dates from a phone between other tasks.

City, nearby towns and the rest of the island

Charlottetown, Stratford and Cornwall sit close together, and customers search for each by name. Many customers also add a street or neighbourhood, so your pages should mention the parts of town you know well. Summerside, Cavendish and other communities are further afield and are searched separately. Someone in Summerside is not looking for a company that only mentions Charlottetown, and the reverse is also true.

Because the island is small, your whole province can be a service area. Customers on the island travel for services, and a company based in the city can reasonably cover nearby towns if the travel makes sense. We still do not make pages for places you will not travel to. See our Moncton page and our Halifax page for the nearest larger Atlantic markets. They show how the same approach adapts to bigger cities with more competition.

Heritage homes and family houses

Older homes in and around downtown call for gentle, careful cleaning, and families across the city want a regular visit they can count on. These customers read your pages slowly and look for signs of care. They want to know who is coming, what products you use and what happens if something is missed.

Show your team, your products and your process. Real photos of your crew, with their permission, beat stock images every time. Our residential cleaning marketing guide explains how to turn that care into pages and reviews that earn recurring clients. A short, friendly page about how a first visit works helps nervous first-time customers say yes.

Inns, restaurants, shops and offices

Commercial work includes accommodation providers, restaurants, small offices and the provincial and institutional buyers that make a capital city what it is. Some buy on a quick call. Others follow a formal process. Many owners of small businesses want the same simple things, which are a reliable crew, a fair quote and proof that you are insured.

A separate commercial page that covers scope, insurance, scheduling and references helps both. Staff turnover, after-hours access and keys are the questions owners ask most, so answer them up front. We keep a simple site visit request form in front of them. Offering a short walk-through request instead of a long contact form suits busy owners. See our guide to office cleaning marketing.

Reviews carry weight on a small island

News travels fast in a small community, and so does a recommendation. A steady stream of recent reviews, with a thoughtful reply to each, builds the kind of trust no advert can buy. Customers often check two or three reviews before they read anything else on your site, so recent ones matter.

We help you ask satisfied customers at the right moment, and we never invent testimonials. A quick text after a finished job, with a direct link to your Google profile, is usually the easiest way. On pricing, we do not publish yours without your agreement. Many owners explain how they quote, covering size, condition and add-ons, rather than listing a number, and that works well. It saves everyone time.

Your profile is your front door

Many customers will meet you first on Google Maps. A complete profile with the right categories, real photos, a full list of services and an honest service area makes a strong first impression. Seasonal changes, such as extended summer hours, should be reflected as soon as they are true. Our guide to a service area Google Business Profile covers the details.

We also update it before the season starts, so hosts searching in spring see current information instead of last year's. We also check categories, services and the description, because small gaps there can hide you from the exact searches you want. Photos from the past season, with the customer's permission, show the work in a way words cannot.

Ads, timed to the calendar

Search ads can be useful ahead of the summer season, when hosts and inn owners are lining up help. They also work for homeowners who search at the start of spring, when deep cleaning is on many minds. We build them around the jobs you actually want and keep the account in your name. We check monthly what each enquiry costs.

Because demand rises and falls so sharply, ads often make sense in bursts rather than all year. We would rather pause spend than pay for clicks you cannot serve. Booking forms, phone numbers and a short explanation of your service area should be on the landing page, so every click has somewhere useful to go. See our Google Ads for cleaning companies page.

Does Prince Edward Island count as one market?

In many ways, yes. Charlottetown is the main search term, but customers elsewhere on the island may also find you. We help you decide which places need their own page and which belong in a simple service area list.

Can you help with cottage and vacation rental cleaning?

Yes. We build a turnover page with a clear scope, a quick quote path and notes on scheduling and access, then set up your Google profile to match. We cannot guarantee bookings, but we can make you easy to choose.

How do I keep work coming in during winter?

Build pages for the work that does not depend on tourists, such as recurring home cleans, offices, restaurants and spring deep cleans. We plan these ahead so your site is ready to catch searches when the season turns.

Do I need pages for Stratford and Cornwall?

Only if you serve them and can add something useful, such as the types of homes or how you schedule visits. Otherwise a clear service area list does the job without looking like filler.

Is it worth investing in SEO on a small island?

Often yes, because fewer cleaners compete and local searches are valuable. A complete profile, clear pages and steady reviews can go a long way. We report what we see each month and never promise a position.

We have a website already. Do we need a new one?

Not necessarily. We review it first. If the structure is sound, we improve pages and fill gaps. If it needs a rebuild, we keep a clean address structure and redirect old pages. That is the heart of good website design for cleaning companies.

Who owns the website and the Google accounts?

You do. The site, the Google Business Profile and the ad account are in your name. If we ever part ways, you keep everything. Ad spend goes straight to Google, and we are added as managers.

How do we get started?

Book a short call or send us an email. We will ask about your services, your seasons and where you work, then send a clear plan and quote. Our questions to ask before hiring an agency can help you compare.

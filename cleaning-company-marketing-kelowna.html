Local guide

Marketing a cleaning company in Kelowna.

A market built around the lake and the seasons

Kelowna runs on a rhythm that is easy to feel. Visitors arrive when the weather warms, rentals fill, patios open and the lakeshore gets busy. The cleaning work follows the same pattern, with a surge of turnovers and deep cleans in summer and a calmer stretch once the crowds leave.

A good plan for cleaning company marketing in Kelowna starts with that rhythm. Pages and profile updates should be ready before the busy season starts, and winter should have its own offers so you keep customers and crews steady through the slower months.

That also means being honest about capacity. If your crew is full in July, it is better to say when you are taking new customers than to leave people waiting. A clear note on availability keeps your reputation intact and your phone calls more useful.

Vacation rentals and the turnover business

Short-term rentals, lakefront suites and holiday homes are a real part of the work here. Hosts want someone who can turn a unit in a few hours, restock supplies, handle linens and send a quick message when it is done. They also want to know you will be there on a busy Saturday.

A page written for hosts, with a short form and a plain explanation of how you schedule and communicate, will do more than a general home cleaning page. See our guide to move-in and move-out cleaning marketing for how similar jobs are presented.

Photos are especially powerful for this audience. A set of before and after shots of a turned-over unit, a stocked linen closet and a tidy deck tell a host what to expect far better than words, and they can be reused on your Google profile.

Lakefront homes, second homes and opening and closing

Many owners of seasonal homes live elsewhere for part of the year. They look for someone who can open the house in spring, close it in fall and check in between visits. If you offer that, say it clearly and describe how you handle keys, alarms and updates to the owner.

It is a small, specific service, and customers who need it often search for it by name. Our residential cleaning marketing guide covers how to present recurring and special-visit services together on one site.

Pricing questions come up often with seasonal work. You do not need to post numbers, but you can explain whether you quote per visit, per hour or per unit, and what a fair estimate depends on, such as size, bedrooms and how quickly it needs to happen.

Neighbourhoods and the wider Okanagan

Kelowna customers describe where they live by area: the Mission, Glenmore, Rutland, the downtown core and the Westside. Across the bridge, West Kelowna and Peachland are their own searches, and Lake Country and Vernon sit farther north.

We build pages where you actually work and have something real to say about the housing and customers there. For the rest, a clear Google service area setup keeps your profile honest without padding the website with thin pages.

Newcomers to the region are an audience of their own. Many do not know local businesses and rely on Google to find someone trustworthy, so reviews, a clear service list and a friendly tone on the About page do much of the persuading for you.

Wineries, clinics, offices and new builds

Growth in the region has brought new offices, medical clinics, shops and many new homes. Commercial buyers want insurance details, references and a written scope, and they want to ask for a quote without a long back and forth. Wineries and tasting rooms add a hospitality side, with weekend and early-morning cleaning needs.

Construction brings its own work. If you handle final cleans for builders, a dedicated page helps contractors find you. See our guides to post-construction cleaning marketing and commercial janitorial marketing.

For builders and property managers, a short case-style description of a typical project, without client names unless you have permission, shows how you plan a final clean, what you handle on site and how you coordinate with trades.

Smoke, dust and the summer fire season

Some summers bring smoke to the valley, and customers start asking about windows, vents, filters and deep cleans afterward. It is a sensitive topic, so be careful what you promise. Explain what you can help with, such as wiping surfaces and cleaning glass, and be honest about what needs a specialist.

Helpful content on this subject can draw people to your site when they are searching. Pair it with a plain note on how you work, and plan around the seasonal marketing calendar so the content is ready before it is needed.

Keep an eye on the weather and your own calendar rather than guessing. Quick updates to a service page or profile post when conditions change keep your information current and help customers who search at the moment they need you.

Reviews, trust and showing your pricing without numbers

In a market with so many visitors and newcomers, people lean heavily on Google reviews. We help you ask happy customers at the right time, reply to every review and keep your photos fresh. We never write reviews or invent testimonials.

On pricing, you do not have to post a number. Many Kelowna companies explain how they quote, covering size, condition, linens, pets and extras. It shows you are open about the process and helps weed out enquiries that are a poor fit.

Asking at the end of a job works best. A short message with a direct link makes leaving a review easy, and a polite reminder a few days later catches people who meant to do it but forgot. Never offer rewards for reviews, as that can break platform rules.

Local SEO, ads and keeping the calendar full

Good marketing for cleaning companies in a seasonal market means being visible before the rush. A tidy profile, strong service pages and a few well-aimed ads for the busy months do more than a constant, unfocused spend.

Our Google Ads work is built around the jobs you most want and the areas you serve, with the account in your name and a monthly look at cost per enquiry. If your site is not ready for paid traffic, we fix that first so you do not pay for clicks that go nowhere.

We also review your numbers each month in plain language. You will see calls, forms and booked jobs, what changed and what we plan to try next, so you always know what your money is doing and why.

How do we get more vacation rental cleaning work in Kelowna?

Build a page written for hosts, explain how you handle turnovers, linens and restocking and add a short quote form. A complete Google profile and good reviews help too. We cannot guarantee bookings, but we can make it easy for hosts to find you.

Is it worth marketing in winter when things are quiet?

Yes. Winter is the time to update your site, collect reviews and promote recurring service, deep cleans and commercial work. It keeps crews steady and gets you ready for the summer rush. We plan content around that rhythm.

Do we need pages for West Kelowna, Lake Country and Vernon?

Only if you genuinely serve them and can say something useful about each. Otherwise a clear service area list is enough. A page that only swaps the town name does not help customers or your search visibility.

How should we talk about wildfire smoke without overpromising?

Stay specific and honest. Explain what you can clean, such as surfaces, windows and vents you are trained to handle, and say when a specialist is needed. Avoid health claims. Helpful, plain content builds trust.

Can you help us find more commercial and construction clients?

Yes. We can build commercial and post-construction pages with your scope, insurance and process, plus a quick form for site visits. We cannot promise contracts, but we can make your offer clear to buyers who search for it.

What does Google Ads look like for a seasonal business?

Usually a modest budget aimed at your best services, with a bigger push before summer and a smaller one in the quiet months. We keep the account in your name and review cost per enquiry monthly. See our pricing page for what we charge.

Who owns the website, Google profile and ad account?

You do. Everything is set up in your name, and we are added as managers. Ad spend goes straight to Google, and you keep every asset if we ever stop working together.

Problems we solve

Where move-in and move-out cleaning companies lose enquiries.

Customers are searching at the last minute

Many move-out enquiries arrive within days of the handover date. If your site does not show availability, a phone number and a fast way to ask, those customers move on to whoever replies first, and you never even know they looked.

The scope is unclear to everyone

People do not know what a move clean should include, so they ask about price first and quality second. Without a published checklist, quotes are hard to compare and you end up in avoidable disagreements about whether the oven or windows were part of the job.

Almost no one hires you twice

Because customers rarely move often, each job feels like starting from zero. Marketing aimed only at individuals keeps you chasing new enquiries, and without referral partners your calendar will swing between packed and empty. A steady stream of referral partners smooths those swings.

Realtors and landlords do not know you exist

The people who send repeat work, such as agents, property managers and landlords, rarely find cleaners by searching. If you have no way to introduce yourself to them, you leave your steadiest source of jobs to chance.

Sellers and renters get the same page

A renter chasing a deposit and a seller prepping for photos have different priorities. One generic page blurs both messages, so neither feels sure that you understand their situation. A line or two for each reader fixes that without doubling the page.

How move-in and move-out cleaning companies win work online

A move-out clean is rarely anyone's idea of a good time. The customer is tired, boxes are everywhere, and a deposit, a handover date or a buyer's inspection is looming. They search at the last minute, often from a phone on moving day eve, and they want somebody who can start tomorrow and finish properly. In that moment, availability and clarity beat polish, so a visible phone number and a plain checklist matter more than clever branding. Be honest about what you can take on that week, and say so.

Customers fall into a few groups, and each needs a slightly different message. Renters worry about their deposit and the landlord's checklist. Sellers want the home to look its best for a showing or final walkthrough. Landlords and property managers need turnovers done between tenants, repeatedly and on a deadline. A site that names these audiences makes each of them feel it was built for them. Even two or three lines for each group, written in their own words, is enough to make the page feel considered rather than copied.

Unlike most cleaning niches, repeat business from the same person is rare. Nobody moves every few months. The real engine is referrals, such as real estate agents, property managers, movers and landlords who send you work again and again. Your marketing therefore has two jobs: catch the person searching today, and quietly build relationships with the people who control a steady stream of moves. That is why a thank-you note, a quick message after a job well done and a simple way to pass your details along matter more than a loyalty offer.

Clear scope is the other big issue. Customers are unsure what a move clean includes, whether inside the oven and cupboards is covered and what the landlord will expect. A page that explains your checklist, notes what you do not do and shows how timing works gets better enquiries and fewer arguments on the day. Include a short list of the extras you offer and what each one adds to the visit, such as inside the fridge or the oven. Customers appreciate knowing the boundaries, and it protects you if a landlord is picky about the final result.

Opportunities

Where the growth is.

Move-out and move-in as separate searches

People search for move out cleaning and move in cleaning as different things. Giving each its own page, with an honest checklist, lets you match both and capture people at either end of a move.

Real estate agent relationships

Agents want a cleaner who is reliable, quick and does not embarrass them in front of a client. A simple page for agents and a tidy introduction can bring consistent referrals before a listing. Offer a printable checklist they can attach to a listing.

Landlord and property manager turnovers

Rental turnovers are predictable, repeatable and often urgent. A page explaining your turnaround, your checklist and how you invoice makes it easy for a manager to add you to their list. Invoicing monthly instead of per job is the sort of detail they notice.

Deposit-focused wording

Renters care about getting their deposit back. Describing what you clean and offering a written checklist they can show the landlord speaks directly to that worry, in a way a general cleaning page never will.

Moving company partnerships

Movers often get asked who can clean afterwards. A short, friendly arrangement to refer one another gives both businesses extra work and costs nothing beyond a conversation. Agree on a simple handoff, such as a card in each other's vans or a shared message thread.

Across Canada

Moving patterns shape this niche more than any other. Student cities see a rush at the end of April and again in late August, when leases turn over and apartments need cleaning in the same few days. Cities with a lot of renters have steady month end demand, while growing suburbs generate move-ins as new builds are completed. Knowing your local rhythm helps you plan staff, marketing and availability. Seasonal swings are worth noting on your pages, because customers like to know you are used to the rush.

Homes differ across the country and so does the work. Older houses in established neighbourhoods have deep window sills, tired cabinets and layers of dust in basements. Newer builds and condos are mostly about construction residue and tight timelines. Towers add elevator bookings and move-out windows set by the building, which changes how you schedule and quote. Describing the kinds of properties you handle helps customers feel you know their place. Those small differences are what make a quote feel local. Mentioning them is quick to do.

Seasons matter in practical ways. In cold areas, salt, slush and mud come in with every box, and winter moves need extra floor attention. Summer brings the busiest weeks and the hardest scheduling. Short term rentals and furnished suites add yet another pattern, with quick turnovers between guests. You can see how this connects with ongoing work on our residential cleaning marketing page. Furnished suites and short term rentals also reward a company that can promise a same day turnaround. Say clearly what you can handle.

What should a move-out cleaning checklist include?

That depends on your service, but most cover kitchens, bathrooms, floors, baseboards, doors, interior windows and the inside of appliances and cupboards. Publish yours and note any extras, such as the oven or fridge interior. A clear list helps customers compare you fairly and cuts down on disagreements when the job is done.

Do I need separate pages for move-in and move-out?

It is worth it. People search for each differently, and the concerns are not the same. Move-out customers worry about deposits and handover dates, while move-in customers want a fresh start before unpacking. Two pages let you match both searches and speak to each worry without crowding a single page.

How do I get referrals from real estate agents?

Be easy to recommend. A short page for agents covering turnaround, access and how you work gives them something simple to forward. A polite, personal note to local agents and a reputation for being on time will do more than any advert. Keep it professional and never push.

Should I market to landlords and property managers?

Yes, they can be your steadiest source. Turnovers repeat, deadlines are real and managers value someone dependable. A page explaining your turnaround, checklist and invoicing helps them add you to their list. Our commercial cleaning page covers how we approach larger accounts and ongoing relationships.

How do I handle last-minute requests?

Make it obvious on the website that you can help quickly, with a phone number visible and a quick form. Behind the scenes, decide how many same-week slots you can honestly take and reply fast. People searching the night before are looking for someone who answers, not the cheapest option available.

Is Google Ads useful for move cleaning?

It often is, because the search is urgent and the intent is clear. Short campaigns in the busy weeks can fill the calendar. Keep the budget modest at first, send clicks to the right move page and measure calls and forms. Pause when you are booked solid, and restart as the next wave approaches.

How should I ask for reviews in a one-time service?

Ask on the day, while the customer is relieved and the home looks great. A short text with a direct link works well. Reviews that mention deposits, handovers or quick turnaround are the most helpful to future customers. Never offer rewards for them, and always reply to what people write.

Can I combine this with regular home cleaning?

Yes, and it is a smart move. A happy move-in customer is a natural prospect for a recurring schedule. We usually keep the move pages separate from regular cleaning, then add a gentle prompt after the job. It is also a good moment to ask for a review and mention your regular plans.
